Du Bois and the people who built public schools

HIST 17B, US History, CCSF · Summer 2026

The part I found most interesting was how Du Bois connects higher education to power, not just to making a living. He says it builds thinkers and citizens, people who think for themselves and have the courage to act on it. What surprised me was how much influence Black people had on public education in the South. They didn’t just gain access to schools, their political power helped build the whole system of public schooling, and white children benefited from it too. Du Bois said it plainly: “by straining his political power to the utmost, the Negro voter got a public school system.” Education was the one thing “no bribery or chicanery could seduce him from advocating.” That kind of determination really moves me. They understood that learning was a condition of freedom, not a luxury.

I keep thinking about this now, when the Department of Education is being gutted by the current administration. People sacrificed so much to build public schooling in the first place. We cannot take it for granted, and communities need to come together and fight back.
