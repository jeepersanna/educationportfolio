Philosophy of education

What I believe about how kids learn, and what schools owe them and their families.

Where it comes from

I learned at home first

I grew up homeschooled in a close church community. My mom was my first teacher. I had a lot of freedom to follow my interests and not much formal teaching, and that mix taught me what freedom gives a kid and what it can't give them alone.

Later, as a parent, I spent years helping run a parent co-op forest school and a weekly play group. When a kid was struggling, our families added support instead of pushing them out. Now I bring those lessons to my kids' public school.

Commitments

What I believe

1

Start from what kids bring

Every child arrives with language, culture, and knowledge from home. Teaching should build on those strengths, not treat them as gaps to fix.

2

Trust comes first

Kids learn from people they trust. Building that relationship is the work, not a warm-up before the work.

3

Families are partners

Kids do better when at least one caregiver is involved. Schools should feel like a place families can go for help, and families should be able to lean on each other there.

4

Home languages are strengths

Bilingualism should be valued, and English learners should be a priority. Honoring a child's first language helps them learn, including in English.

5

Discipline should mean care

No isolating kids, and never taking away lunch or recess. A call home should mean someone cares about you, not that you're in trouble. Labels like "bad kid" stick, so impact matters more than intent.

6

Social emotional learning is learning

Kids need help naming feelings, working through conflict, and repairing harm. I've helped write community guidelines grounded in social emotional learning and social justice, and I want that in every classroom.

I want schools where families can lean on each other, so we can all be there for all kids.
