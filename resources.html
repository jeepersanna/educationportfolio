Family and community

In a crisis: who to call besides the police

Calling 911 brings police, and for many families that makes a hard moment more dangerous. These lines are staffed by counselors and peers, not officers. Most are free, 24 hours, and have interpreters.

  • Comprehensive Crisis Services: 628-217-7000San Francisco’s own 24-hour mental health crisis team. Counselors who can talk by phone or come to you. All SF crisis lines (SF.gov).
  • SF Suicide Prevention: 415-781-050024 hours. Confidential. Someone to talk to when things feel unbearable.
  • Peer Warm Line: 855-600-927624 hours. For when it isn’t an emergency and you just need to talk to a person who has been there. About the Warm Line.
  • Street Crisis Response TeamCall 311 and ask for the Street Team. Health workers, not police, respond to someone in a mental health or drug crisis on the street.
  • Trans Lifeline: 877-565-8860Peer support by and for trans people, 2 to 10 p.m. They will not send police or emergency services unless you ask them to. translifeline.org.
  • 988 Suicide and Crisis LifelineCall or text, 24 hours, in many languages. Good to know: 988 counselors can send emergency services in rare cases they consider life-threatening, so ask about their policy if that matters to you.

Someone in immediate physical danger still needs 911. The point of this list is that most crises are not that, and you deserve help that doesn’t put your family at risk.

Domestic violence and sexual assault

Confidential. You don’t have to leave, press charges, or involve police to call. Counselors help you think through safety, housing, kids, and next steps, at your pace.

  • W.O.M.A.N., Inc.: 877-384-357824 hours. Counseling, safety planning, and referrals for anyone experiencing domestic violence. womaninc.org.
  • La Casa de las Madres: 877-503-185024 hours, in Spanish and English. Teens can call 877-923-0700. Shelter, support, and help for parents and kids. lacasa.org.
  • Asian Women’s Shelter: 877-751-088024 hours, with interpreters in many Asian languages. sfaws.org.
  • Black Women Revolt Against Domestic Violence: 888-260-1498Monday to Friday, noon to 4 p.m. Support by and for Black survivors.
  • SF Women Against Rape: 415-647-727324 hours. Support for survivors of sexual assault, whenever it happened. sfwar.org.
  • Community United Against Violence (CUAV)Support and safety planning for LGBTQ people experiencing violence, including from partners or family.
  • All SF crisis lines for survivors (SF.gov)The city’s full list.

Immigrant families

Every child can go to public school no matter their status. Schools in San Francisco don’t ask. These are the networks that respond when ICE shows up, and that help families plan ahead.

  • SF Rapid Response Network: 415-200-154824 hours. Call to report ICE activity, get a trained observer sent out, or get legal help for someone detained. Run by community groups, not the city. SFILEN is the network of legal and community organizations behind it.
  • Red cards (Immigrant Legal Resource Center)Free wallet cards, in many languages, that state your rights if ICE comes to your door. Print them, hand them out at pickup.
  • SFUSD: Rights of undocumented studentsThe district’s safe haven policy, and what happens at school if a parent is detained. Keep your child’s emergency card up to date.
  • SF.gov: Help with immigration enforcementThe city’s page: what to do, who to call, and free legal services.
  • Office of Civic Engagement and Immigrant AffairsCity office for immigrant services, language access, and know-your-rights workshops.
  • National Day Laborer Organizing NetworkTrains neighbors to watch for ICE and support each other. Their “ICE Out of Our Communities” trainings are how many of us learned.

Mental health and wellness

For ongoing support, not only a crisis. The crisis lines are in the section above.

  • SF Behavioral Health access line: 888-246-333324 hours. Help finding mental health and substance use care, including with Medi-Cal and benefits. SF.gov: Get help for mental health or substance use.
  • Mental Health & Wellness Community Resource ListHotlines, youth and teen support, family services, and grief support in SF. Compiled by the Filipino Mental Health Initiative of San Francisco (FMHI-SF), a program of the Filipino American Development Foundation.
