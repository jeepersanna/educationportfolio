Who gets a say?

Structural barriers to family participation in a multilingual public school

An interview study of who actually shapes decisions at a multilingual public elementary school, through ELAC, the School Site Council, and the PTA. I found that participation is filtered by language, networks, scheduling, and citizenship, and that families’ limited voice reflects a failure of institutional design, not a lack of interest or effort.

Common ground: third places on Ocean Avenue

Immigrant entrepreneurship, community space, and the coffee shops of Ocean Avenue

Three immigrant-rooted cafés share one street, each founded with a mission to build community, not just sell coffee. I compared how each one works as a third place. I found that third places are built on purpose, through design, owners who show up and know people’s names, and time, and that the market can threaten what a mission built.

Where do kids get to just exist?

Where can a kid in San Francisco spend unsupervised time that doesn't cost money, and what has disappeared over the last few decades?

This project takes the third place idea from my Ocean Avenue study and asks it about children and teens. Adults have cafes, bars, and barbershops. Kids mostly have school, home, and whatever adults schedule for them. I want to map what's left in my part of the city and hear from parents and kids about where they actually go.

It sits where my two interests meet: sociology and children's liberation.

Literacy, critical thinking, and home languages

I want to understand how kids and teens become strong readers and critical thinkers, and who gets taught to read well. Three threads pull me most:

  • Reading development. How reading works, and why some kids struggle.
  • Critical literacy. Questioning who wrote a text and why, including media and information literacy.
  • Equity and policy. Curriculum fights, and which kids get the best teaching.

Home languages run through all of it. I believe English learners should be a priority, and that honoring a child's home language and valuing bilingualism serves them better than a narrow push on English. I'm also interested in programs where parents grow their own education alongside their kids.
